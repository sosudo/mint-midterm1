Let $E,F\subseteq\R^N$ be such that $d := \operatorname{dist}(E,F)\gt0$.

claim $(\mathcal L^N)^*(E\cup F)\ge(\mathcal L^N)^*(E)+(\mathcal L^N)^*(F)$.

Note that since $(\mathcal L^N)^*$ is subadditive, if the claim holds then $(\mathcal L^N)^*(E\cup F) = (\mathcal L^N)^*(E)+(\mathcal L^N)^*(F)$. (see Def 3.11)Subadditivity always gives $\le$; the claim gives $\ge$.

Consider a sequence $\{R_n\}_{n\in\N}$ of rectangles such that $$E\cup F\subseteq\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty R_n.$$

By partitioning each rectangle $R_n$ into smaller rectangles, if necessary, we can assume that $\operatorname{diam}R_n\lt\frac d2$ $\forall n\in\N$.Cut each side of $R_n$ into $m$ equal pieces: $R_n$ becomes $m^N$ subrectangles of diameter $\operatorname{diam}(R_n)/m$, and their volumes add up exactly to $\ell(R_n)$ (grid additivity, as in Prop 2.11). Choose $m$ large. Replacing each $R_n$ by its pieces (still countably many rectangles in total) keeps the cover and its total cost.

Hence, if $R_n\cap E\ne\emptyset\Rightarrow R_n\cap F = \emptyset$; $R_n\cap F\ne\emptyset\Rightarrow R_n\cap E = \emptyset$.If $R_n$ met both, pick $x\in R_n\cap E$ and $y\in R_n\cap F$: then $d(x,y)\le\operatorname{diam}R_n\lt\frac d2\lt d = \operatorname{dist}(E,F)$, contradicting the definition of $\operatorname{dist}$ as an infimum over such pairs. Therefore, we can divide the sequence $\{R_n\}$ into two sequences, one covering $E$, and the other covering $F$. It follows that

$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty\ell(R_n)\ge\sum_{R_n\cap E\ne\emptyset}\ell(R_n)+\sum_{R_n\cap F\ne\emptyset}\ell(R_n)\ge(\mathcal L^N)^*(E)+(\mathcal L^N)^*(F).$$

First: by the previous step the two index sets are disjoint, so the two sums are parts of the full sum (rectangles meeting neither are just dropped).

Second: the rectangles meeting $E$ cover $E$ (every point of $E$ is in some $R_n$, which then meets $E$), so their total volume is $\ge(\mathcal L^N)^*(E)$ by definition of the infimum; same for $F$.

Taking the infimum over all sequences $\{R_n\}$ covering $E\cup F$ give $$(\mathcal L^N)^*(E\cup F)\ge(\mathcal L^N)^*(E)+(\mathcal L^N)^*(F),$$ proving the claim. ■

4. Measures

Let $(X,\M)$ be a measurable space. A measure on $(X,\M)$ is a function $\mu:\M\to[0,+\infty]$ such that

  1. $\mu(\emptyset) = 0$,
  2. (countably additive) $\displaystyle\mu\Big(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty E_n\Big) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty\mu(E_n)$ for every family $\{E_n\}\in\M$ of pairwise disjoint sets.

$\boxed{(X,\M,\mu)}$ is called a measure space.

a) Counting measure on a set $X$, with $\M = \mathcal P(X)$: $$\mu(E) = \begin{cases}\#E & \text{if } E \text{ is finite, (\# of elems. of } E)\\ +\infty & \text{—}.\end{cases}$$

b) Dirac measure. Let $(X,\M)$ be a measurable space, and let $a_0\in X$. $$\delta_{a_0}(E) := \begin{cases}1 & \text{if } a_0\in E,\\ 0 & \text{if } a_0\notin E\end{cases},\quad E\in\M.$$

c) Let $X\ne\emptyset$ and consider the σ-algebra $\M$ of all subsets of $X$ that are either countable or have a countable complement in $X$. Set $$\mu(E) := \begin{cases}0 & \text{if } E \text{ is countable},\\ 1 & \text{if } E \text{ is uncountable},\end{cases}\quad E\in\M.$$

Let $(X,\M,\mu)$ be a measure space, and let $E_n\in\M$ be such that $E_n\supseteq E_{n+1}$, $\forall n\in\N$, and $\mu(E_1)\lt+\infty$. Then $$\mu\Big(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n\Big) = \lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu(E_n).$$

By De Morgan's law $$E_1\setminus\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty(E_1\setminus E_n),\ \text{with}\ E_1\setminus E_1\subseteq E_1\setminus E_2\subseteq\dots\subseteq E_1\setminus E_n\subseteq\dots$$A point of $E_1$ fails to be in all $E_n$ iff it is outside some $E_n$. The sets $E_1\setminus E_n$ grow because the $E_n$ shrink.

By Prop 4.6., $$\mu\Big(E_1\setminus\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n\Big) = \lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu(E_1\setminus E_n),\tag{$*$}$$ and by monotonicity and the fact that $\mu(E_1)\lt+\infty$, we have $\mu\big(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n\big),\ \mu(E_n)\lt+\infty$ for all $n\in\N$.All these sets are subsets of $E_1$.

Therefore, by Prop 4.3 (ii) $(*)$ can be written as $$\cancel{\mu(E_1)}-\mu\Big(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n\Big) = \lim_{n\to+\infty}\big[\cancel{\mu(E_1)}-\mu(E_n)\big],$$Apply Prop 4.3 (ii) on both sides (the subtracted sets have finite measure), then cancel the finite number $\mu(E_1)$, which also passes through the limit. and so $$\mu\Big(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty E_n\Big) = \lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu(E_n).\ \blacksquare$$

Given a measure space $(X,\M,\mu)$, the measure $\mu$ is said to be complete if for every $E\in\M$ with $\mu(E) = 0$ it follows that every $F\subseteq E$ also belongs to $\M$. [equivalently, we say that $\M$ is complete with respect to $\mu$].
